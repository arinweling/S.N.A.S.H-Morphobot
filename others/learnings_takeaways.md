## Manegerial Learnings
#### Things learnt the hard way:
1. This project highlights the importance of team management and coordination. Irrespective of how good we are at such stuff individually, it is the complete team effort that will pay off in the end. We understood the need for proper and frequent communication in the team. There should be clear and justified work allotment to each group member. Additionally each member should be equally responsible and answerable for the project and its outcome. The success of the project is a team effort.
2. There is a need for proper measurement, simulations, verification and testing before actually handling things physically. We made multiple errors in design due to both internal and external factors. The lost time, effort and resources due to the misalignments has made us realise this point strongly. The incorporation of proper Proof of Concept testing enabled us to rectify our mistakes on time.
3. This course is certainly not a cake walk for sure. We need to give a significant amount of time and effort to this course. Thus, time management becomes very important. We need to balance this course with other courses, commitments, etc, along with keeping leeway for any mishaps/ delays that might happen. By the end of this course, the other commitments got sidelined. This highlights the need for proper planning on a daily, weekly and monthly basis.

#### Things learnt the easy way:
1. This project was possibly largely because all of us had the passion to work on the given theme. We all have some sort of inclination towards robotics and were excited to build the morphobot. This gave us a push throughout the course and kept us motivated to complete the project for our own happiness and satisfaction.
2. Proper research and understanding is a must in any kind of project. It is important to gain a good idea of what the project is about and to have a clear understanding of what is expected from ourselves in the project. This once again requires proper communication and coordination in the team.
3. Frequent reviews help a lot. The Milestone submissions every fortnight played an integral role for us to stay on track most of the time. The meaningful reviews and criticism which we got from the professors, lab RAs, TAs, staff, peers, etc. helped us realise if we met the expected outcomes or not. They helped us mitigate our difficulties.

## Technical Learnings
Since most of us had a lot of experience in tech teams, there weren't any major technical takeaways but a few important ones are written below:

1. We thought of using a quick iterative fail-fast approach for our leg design. Almost 7-8 iterations of the leg were designed and 3D printed during the second half of the project. Each leg design was an improvement upon the previous and allowed us to test how well it works in real life rather than wait and simulate. Though it was largely successful with a nice design at the end, we did end up wasting quite a lot of material and perhaps made a little too many iterations. The iterative process should have entailed more anaylsis between each new version.
2. Learn how to solder properly. It is a very very underrated skill for electrical engineers. No matter how well you made the design, it is the final execution that matters and soldering is an integral part of it. Majority of the problems related to PCB's are due to poor soldering only.
3. 3D printing is not entirely reliable. It is very important to look at the clearances kept and some basic stress analysis. Our shaft kept breaking off the 3D printed part due to a design issue which we finally fixed with a seal.
